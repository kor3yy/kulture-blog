For Savia Ashby, music has always been connected to identity. Long before she was writing songs or performing, she was shaped by the values, cultures, and experiences that surrounded her growing up. Now, the Toronto-based singer-songwriter is bringing those influences into her own version of pop – one built around strong vocals, honest songwriting, and a sound that refuses to stay in one category.

With her releases But I Might and Juiceboxx, Ashby has already shown two different sides of herself as an artist. One explores vulnerability, while the other embraces confidence and personality. Together, they introduce an artist who is comfortable being both emotionally open and unapologetically herself.

![Savia Ashby at the beach](/images/beach.jpg)

*Savia Ashby in Toronto.*

## Roots and Identity

Growing up in Canada, Ashby describes her childhood as a supportive and communicative environment. As an only child in a family of three, she was raised in a home where she felt respected and included. That sense of support became one of the foundations behind her confidence – not only as a person but as an artist.

Ashby's identity is also shaped by the combination of cultures she grew up around. Her mother's side is Polish, while her father's side is Canadian with Scandinavian roots. Rather than feeling separate, those backgrounds naturally blended together throughout her upbringing. Her father's Canadian influence reflected the environment she lived in, while her mother kept Polish traditions present in their family life.

![Savia Ashby with a red car](/images/red-car-cutie.jpg)

For Ashby, that connection to her heritage continues to influence the way she sees the world.

> "Through the Polish-European lens, I have very strong values and I'm proud of my country," she explains. "I feel as though it has made me more resilient and given me a strong connection to other Polish people around me."

Those values don't stop at her personal life – they find their way into her music. When asked what parts of herself appear most in her work, Ashby points to the qualities she has carried with her from childhood.

> "My strength and resilience does for sure, my confidence as well 100%."

## Finding Music

Although singing was always something she loved, music became something more serious when she was 15 years old. That was the point where it shifted from something she enjoyed into something she wanted to pursue as a career.

Growing up, Ashby found inspiration in some of pop's biggest names, including Ariana Grande, Selena Gomez, Lady Gaga, and The Black Eyed Peas. Those artists shaped her appreciation for memorable melodies, powerful performances, and music that connects with people.

![Savia Ashby wearing a cap](/images/wearing-a-cap-pretty.jpg)

However, inspiration does not mean imitation. Ashby is focused on taking those influences and turning them into something personal.

She describes her sound as music for people who want to feel good – confident, energetic, and built around emotion. Her current direction combines dark pop, EDM influences, rock elements, and pop production, while keeping vocals at the center of everything.

## The Creative Process

When creating a song, the process begins with meaning.

> "First I think of my concept – what is it that I really wanna say? Then I find a beat and vibe, and then I write the lyrics. Production ideas happen as I'm in the studio and we hear cool stuff. We pivot all the time."

That willingness to experiment is an important part of Ashby's creative process. Instead of forcing a song into a specific formula, she allows ideas to develop naturally and lets the music evolve along the way.

She describes her music as "very fun and vocally focused," a style influenced by the singers she admired growing up, especially Ariana Grande and her ability to combine technical skill with emotion.

## But I Might and Juiceboxx

![But I Might single artwork](/images/but-i-might.jpg)

That vocal focus is already present throughout Ashby's releases. But I Might introduced listeners to a more vulnerable side of her artistry, using emotion and honesty as the foundation. The song showed her ability to express personal experiences while creating something relatable.

*But I Might, Savia Ashby's debut single.*

![Juiceboxx single artwork](/images/juiceboxx.jpg)

With Juiceboxx, Ashby shifted the energy. The track brings a more playful and confident perspective, using humor and clever lyricism to explore modern dating and emotional immaturity. Instead of moving away from vulnerability, it shows another side of the same artist – one who can be both reflective and fearless.

*Juiceboxx, Ashby's second single.*

In a genre where many artists can begin to blend together, Ashby believes her voice and songwriting approach help separate her from the crowd.

> "I can perform very well vocally, and I think my voice has a uniqueness to it. Also lyrically, I try to be innovative."

## Looking Ahead

Looking ahead, Ashby sees herself somewhere between the worlds of artists like Madison Beer and Ariana Grande, while still creating something that reflects her own perspective.

> "I think I'll fall into the vein of the more Madison Beer and Ariana Grande route, but I do think I'll create a new section in that area by mixing the dark pop, EDM, vocals, and rock elements."

Beyond her releases, Ashby continues building her presence through performances in Toronto and connecting with listeners online through behind-the-scenes studio content, previews, and creative videos that show the personality behind the music.

![Savia Ashby modeling](/images/savia-being-a-model.jpg)

For Ashby, the goal has never been simply to make songs – it has been to create something people can connect with. But I Might introduced her emotional side. Juiceboxx showed her confidence. Together, they represent the beginning of an artist who knows where she wants to go while still leaving room to discover what comes next.

![Savia Ashby tunnel vision](/images/tunnel-vision.jpg)

Savia Ashby isn't trying to fit into pop music's existing blueprint. She's building one that feels like her own.
